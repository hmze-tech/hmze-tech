Ilya Sakharov is Executive Director of Engineering at Redcare Pharmacy, the largest e-pharmacy in Europe. He started in quality and testing, moved through platform engineering, SRE and developer productivity at Microsoft, RSA, HelloFresh and Codility, and still describes his job as one question with increasingly expensive answers: how do engineers move faster without setting production on fire.

In this episode we go past tooling. Ilya argues that the real risk of AI is not worse code but thinner understanding: cognitive debt piling up on top of technical debt, ownership without the ability to explain, and organisations that outsource the thinking in the middle while keeping full accountability for the result. We test that claim from both sides. André pushes back with the abstraction argument, Sebastian brings in the calculator analogy, and Ilya explains why neither holds this time. Along the way we cover the state of AI adoption at Redcare, why he refuses to call any of it automation, why the organisation is splitting into builders and consumers of AI systems, and why platform engineering now serves marketing, finance and legal as much as it serves engineers.

The reality check is a GitHub Copilot agent that was tagged to answer a question about tests and decided to rewrite the function instead. The prediction is the era of disposable code: architecture as the only constant, regeneration instead of builds and CI/CD, and eventually a language machines invent for themselves that we will not read.
